=== PAGE 8 ===
Some different areas of accounting
• Financial - prepare financial statements as well as analyse and interpret financial
information.
• Managerial - produce, analyse and communicate information to managers to
assist internal users in making well informed business decisions e.g. budgeting,
pricing and planning for future needs.
• Cost - control the costs of producing goods and services.
• Taxation - preparation of tax returns and tax payments
• Auditing – review to provide assurance that Financial Statements provide a true
and fair view

=== PAGE 9 ===
Book-keeping Vs Accounting
Book-keeping
•
The systematic recording of daily transactions,
such as sales, purchases, and expenses.
•
Generally, involves routine tasks and
straightforward data entry.
•
Maintains the financial data and ensures that it
is complete and accurate.
•
Typically requires basic accounting knowledge
and may not require advanced qualifications.
Accounting
•
Broader range of activities: summarizing,
analyzing, reporting financial data to provide
insights and strategic information, interpreting
and analyzing the financial records.
•
More complex tasks: adjusting entries, financial
analysis, and compliance with accounting
standards.
•
Uses the financial data to prepare reports,
conduct a
•
Formal education: a degree in accounting or
finance, certification (e.g., CPA).

=== PAGE 11 ===
Users of Accounting Information
1. Owners/shareholders/managers - assess the financial position
and performance and make informed decisions.
2. Investors - assess investment potential.
3. Lenders/banks - assess whether the line of credit is sound.
4. Tax authorities - assess tax liabilities.
5. Competitors - assess performance against that of the
business in the same Industry.

=== PAGE 12 ===
Requirements of Accounting Reports
•
Clarity (Understandability) – comprehendible and clearly presented with the use of
headings and subheadings.
•
Accuracy (Reliability) - The information should correspond with the actual
underlying transactions and events, and is capable of independent verification and is
free from bias, not misleading.
•
Consistency (Comparability) - identify similarities and differences between the
current information and information from previous years' reports or reports of
different entities.
•
Timely - reports should be prepared in plenty of time for the people requiring the
information to make decisions.
•
Compliance - comply with any regulation eg accounting standards

=== PAGE 14 ===
1. Accounting Cycle: Transactions
•
Transactions are events that alter the financial position of a
business and require the entering of information in accounting
records
•
Generally, this could involve activities of:
1. Buying
2. Selling
3. Exchanging of goods and services for cash or on credit.
Only 2 ways:  1. Cash
                   2. Credit (pay later)

=== PAGE 15 ===
2. Accounting Cycle: Source Documents
•
The evidence/proof of a transaction occurring
•
Capture and contain details of the transaction that will be recorded into the
journals
•
Examples of Source documents:
a)
Invoice : issued by seller when goods are sold on credit
b)
Credit Note : issued by seller goods are returned by the buyer due to
damage, wrong size etc.
c)
Receipt : Issued by seller when payment is received to confirm payment
has been collected

=== PAGE 16 ===
Accounting Cycle – Journal, Ledger, Trial Balance
3. Journal: Journals are the first book of entry that are used to record details
and information from source documents and identify the accounts affected by
a transaction
4. Ledger: The ledger is a book of secondary entry and contains all the
accounts of a business.
• T-format
• Column Format
5. Trial Balance: The Trial Balance is a list of all the account balances in the
General Ledger (after the balancing process for each account is completed).

=== PAGE 17 ===
6. Accounting Cycle: Reports/Financial Statements
•
The Profit and Loss Statement is also known as the Income Statement
or the Statement of Financial Performance
•
The Balance Sheet or the Statement of Financial Position displays the
assets, liabilities and owner’s equity of the business at a point in time.
•
The Cash Flow Statement shows the inflow and outflow of cash from the
business during an accounting period.

=== PAGE 5 ===
General Ledger
5
• Definition: A General Ledger (GL) is a comprehensive record of all
financial transactions within a company. It is the accounting record from
which financial statements are prepared.
• All the accounts are in the General Ledger
• Purpose: To provide a complete record of all financial transactions,
ensuring accuracy and completeness in financial reporting.

=== PAGE 6 ===
General Ledger: Function
6
Recording Transactions
•
Transactions are initially recorded in the journal (Journal Entries).
•
Entries are then posted to the respective accounts in the General Ledger.
Debits and Credits
•
Each transaction affects at least two accounts on each side: Dr & Cr
•
While posting in the General Ledger, Debit posts must equal to the Credit
posts.

=== PAGE 25 ===
T-Format balancing
1. Periodic balancing is required (usually monthly/at the end of the
accounting period)
2. Begin on the larger side:
•
Debit side for Assets & Expenses
•
Credit side for Liabilities, Owner’s Equity and Revenue
3. Write down the larger total on both sides. Write Balance c/d
(closing balance) on the smaller side (end of current month)

=== PAGE 26 ===
T-Format balancing
4. Calculate the balance on the smaller side at the end of the period, called Balance c/d. This is
also the Closing Balance of the account, as this is what you are closing your business with at
the end of the accounting period. c/d = carried down
5. Record Balance b/d on the opposite side, showing the balance that you are beginning your
business with. Hence, also known as the Opening Balance (start of next month). b/d = brought
down
6. Opening balances/Balance b/d  for Assets, Expenses Drawings – Debit. That is why they are
called Debit Accounts
7. Opening balances/balance b/d for Liabilities, OE, Revenue – Credit. That is why they are
called Credit Accounts

=== PAGE 27 ===
27
27
Note: T format balancing
• If the debit side total > credit
side total, the balance is called
a debit balance
• A debit balance must be
brought down (b/d) to the
debit side of the account at the
start of the next month
• If the credit side total >
debit side total, the
balance is called a credit
balance
• A credit balance must be
brought down (b/d) to the
credit side of the account
at the start of the next
month

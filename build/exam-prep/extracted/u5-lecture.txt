=== PAGE 4 ===
Why do we need Adjusting Entries?
4
•
Only arises when accrual-based accounting is adopted
•
 Accrual-based, in some cases the period in which cash is paid or received
does not coincide with the period in which expense and income are
recognised.
•
Therefore, some accounts must be adjusted on the last day of the
accounting period to correctly recognise expenses and income not
reflected in cash receipts or payments.
Never adjusts cash account !

=== PAGE 8 ===
Prepayments: Prepaid Expenses
8
•
The payment of expenses in advance
•
Cash paid before benefits are consumed/expired (future benefit)
•
Initially recorded as a current asset at time of payment
•
Over time, the future benefits are consumed/expired. This
consumed/expired portion needs to be recorded as an expense.
•
E.g. prepaid insurance, prepaid rent
Initial transaction
Adjusting Entry
Prepaid Expense – Dr (Asset)
Expense - Dr
Cash - Cr
Prepaid Expense – Cr (Asset)

=== PAGE 13 ===
•
Depreciation is the reduction in value of a non-current asset over time, normally
brought about by the use of the asset.
•
This includes wear or obsolescence over the life.
•
Depreciation is an Expense and calculated for each accounting period.
▪
Adjusting entry:
▪
Dr – Depreciation Expense
▪
   Cr – Accumulated Depreciation         this account is used to reduce the value
                                                   of the asset – application of historical cost convention.
Prepayments: Depreciation

=== PAGE 17 ===
Accruals : Accrued Revenue
17
•
Revenues that have been earned in the period, but payment has not yet been
received
•
Revenue must be recognised along with a receivable account (for a future
receipt of cash)
•
E.g. of accounts : accounts receivable, interest receivable
Adjusting entry:
Dr – Receivable (asset)

Cr – Revenue (service/product provided therefore revenue increases)

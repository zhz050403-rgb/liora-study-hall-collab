Monetary Unit Convention
Assumes that all financial information will be described the
currency of the operating country.
For example:
• in Dollars and Cents for Australian purposes.
• in Yen for Japanese purposes.
• In Pounds for UK purposes

=== PAGE 10 ===
Accounting Period Convention
• The indefinite life of the business is split into regular intervals.
    usually 12 months or 1 year (eg 1 July 2020 to 30 June 2021).
• financial period = accounting period = reporting period.
• This allows the business activities and performance to be
measured, enable an assessment of the results and a meaningful
comparison with expected or past results.

=== PAGE 14 ===
Accrual Convention
14
• This convention ensures that financial statements reflect all relevant income
and expenses within the period in which they occur, providing a more
accurate picture of a company’s financial performance and position.
• The matching of Revenues and Expenses for the defined accounting
period is an important part of profit/loss measurement and the subsequent
preparation of the Income Statement and Balance Sheet.
• Expenses are recognized when they are incurred, not when
cash is paid.
• Revenues are recognized when they are earned, not when
cash is received

=== PAGE 16 ===
Consistency Convention
16
•
Once an organization adopts a particular accounting method or practice, it
should consistently apply it in future periods unless a change is warranted
and justified.
•
Consistency in accounting methods allows for accurate comparison of
financial statements over time, enhancing the reliability and comparability of
financial information.
•
This convention helps ensure that
•
financial statements are comparable across different periods,
•
providing stakeholders with a clearer view of the organization’s financial
performance and position.

=== PAGE 17 ===
Conservatism
17
This convention aims to provide a more cautious and prudent view of a
company's financial position, minimizing the risk of overstatement of assets and
income.
•
Revenue Recognition Principle: Revenues and profits should only be
recognized when they are realized and there is a high level of certainty,
avoiding the premature recognition of income.
•
Expense and Loss Recognition: Expenses and losses should be
recognized as soon as they are probable or foreseeable, even if they have
not yet been incurred, to ensure that financial statements do not overstate
financial health.
